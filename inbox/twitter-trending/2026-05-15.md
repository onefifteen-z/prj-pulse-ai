### 🔥 Agentic frameworks crack hard science: physics-intern (CritPt SOTA) + AEvo (agentic self-improvement)

- **Mentioned by**: Thom_Wolf, dair_ai (amplifier)
- **Posts on X**: https://x.com/Thom_Wolf/status/2054608252344689083, https://x.com/dlouapre/status/2054217281895309480, https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2055000283605004602
- **Summary**: Thom Wolf describes the *vibe* of watching agent teams tackle hard theoretical physics — self-correcting, deriving equations, re-estimating approaches — and the underlying **physics-intern** framework lifts Gemini 3.1 Pro from **17.7% → 31.4% on CritPt**, a new SOTA on a notoriously hard LLM benchmark, by decomposing problems across specialized agents. Same week, dair_ai surfaces **AEvo (Harnessing Agentic Evolution)**: split the self-improvement loop into a *proposer* and a *meta-agent* that watches what worked, what failed, and edits the procedure that proposes future candidates; past runs (candidates, feedback, traces, failures) become memory for the meta-agent. **+26% relative gain over the strongest evolution baseline** on agentic and reasoning benchmarks; SOTA on three open-ended tasks.
- **Quote** (Wolf on the qualitative experience):

<p>watching a team of agents tackling a hard theoretical physics problem is quite mesmerizing - self-correcting, deriving hard equations, computing intermediate results, re-estimating the best approach</p>

- **Quote** (AEvo's loop in one line):

<p>One proposes the next candidate.<br>
<br>
The other watches what worked, what failed, and edits the procedure that proposes future candidates.</p>

- **Why it matters**: Two independent results converging on the same recipe — *asynchronous multi-specialist + memory of failures + meta-procedure that edits the proposer*. For anyone running iterative agentic search loops (research, codegen, eval), AEvo is the architectural template to copy; physics-intern is the proof that the same template moves SOTA on expert problems.

### 🔥 Frozen-model tricks: Lighthouse Attention (Nous) + δ-mem online memory

- **Mentioned by**: rasbt, dair_ai (amplifier)
- **Posts on X**: https://x.com/rasbt/status/2054543968344412621, https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2054600147020222630
- **Summary**: Sebastian Raschka highlights **Lighthouse Attention** from Nous Research — a subquadratic wrapper used during *most* of pretraining and then *removed* before deployment, recovering vanilla full-attention quality at much cheaper long-context training. dair_ai amplifies **δ-mem**, a fixed-size online associative-memory state that augments a *frozen* full-attention backbone with low-rank attention corrections (no fine-tuning, no backbone swap, no context extension); an 8×8 state lifts average score by **1.10×**, with **1.31× on MemoryAgentBench** and **1.20× on LoCoMo**.
- **Quote** (Raschka on the operational appeal):

<p>Interesting paper. What I like about this is that it is a relatively low-commitment attention modification.<br>
<br>
I.e., one can use it during most of training, switch back to vanilla attention near the end, and recover roughly the same modeling performance as if full attention had been used the whole time.</p>

- **Why it matters**: Both results push the "do more with the model you already have" frontier — train cheaper, deploy frozen, bolt memory on. The cost story for infra/research teams is shifting from "scale context window" to "augment with side state and removable wrappers." Worth a quick eng-side eval if you're running long-context or memory-heavy stacks.

### 🔥 Multi-agent failure modes & the "agentic AI as path to AGI" thesis

- **Mentioned by**: dair_ai (3 posts; aggregator-only)
- **Posts on X**: https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2055000300545785937, https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2054547408529530980, https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2055000283605004602
- **Summary**: Three dair_ai threads, all in 24 hours, sketching one composite thesis: (1) a position paper arguing **agentic AI**, not bigger foundation models alone, is the most foreseeable route to AGI — formalizing what "agentic" contributes (memory, reasoning, tool use, self-improvement, alignment) as separable axes with their own bottlenecks. (2) **AEvo** (above) — a concrete instantiation of the self-improvement axis. (3) **Sovereignty Gap** — 22,500 deterministic trajectories across GAIA, SWE-bench, and Multi-Challenge with three frontier models showing agents *frequently compute the correct answer internally, then suppress it to agree with the swarm*. Order of agents in the pipeline matters more than count.
- **Why it matters**: Aggregator-only (single account, weight 2 each), so confidence is moderate. But the cluster is internally coherent: *the bottleneck isn't model size, it's how agents update each other and their procedures.* If you build multi-agent systems in production, the Sovereignty Gap finding is the operational warning ("you may be manufacturing alignment hallucinations at scale"); AEvo is the partial fix.
