### 🔥 Agent evals are having a credibility week — "Evaluation Trap" meets a cognition→action tool-call gap

- **Mentioned by**: dair_ai (2 fresh posts 5/16; cluster with 5/15 multi-agent + grep threads)
- **Posts on X** (canonical): https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2055747638381920342, https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2055750162526715926, https://x.com/dair_ai/status/2055318564127809571, https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2055317577031975269, https://x.com/omarsar0/status/2055000300545785937
- **Summary**: In 24 hours dair_ai surfaced two papers that attack *different* failure modes of the same underlying problem — we don't know what agent benchmarks actually measure, and even when models "know" they should call a tool they often don't. **"The Evaluation Trap"** (arxiv 2605.14167) coins vocabulary for auditing whether a benchmark discriminates the underlying capability or just correlates with proxy behaviors — and argues most agent leaderboards are not measuring what practitioners think. Separately, an interpretability paper on tool-using agents (2605.14038) finds a **26–54% mismatch** between "model should call tool" (decodable in hidden states) and actual tool calls; the gap concentrates in the **cognition→action transition**, where late-layer geometry rotates the probe signal nearly orthogonal to the emitted action. That pairs with the carryover **"Is Grep All You Need?"** thread (grep harness ≈ embedding retrieval on coding tasks) and the **Beyond Individual Intelligence** multi-agent survey (self-evolution as the cleanest map of memory / meta-learning / procedure-editing). Aggregator-only, but internally coherent: the bottleneck is *measurement + harness + late-layer action routing*, not another pretraining scale-up.
- **Quote** (Evaluation Trap, via dair_ai):

<p>Are your benchmarks actually measuring the capability you think they measure?<br>
<br>
New paper says they probably not.<br>
<br>
Coined the "The Evaluation Trap", it provides a vocabulary for auditing whether your eval discriminates the underlying capability or just proxies behaviors that happen to correlate.<br>
<br>
Most benchmarks bake in implicit theory that nobody states explicitly, then evaluate as if the theory were neutral.</p>

- **Quote** (tool-use cognition→action gap, via omarsar0):

<p>The authors probe hidden states and find the model often recognizes it should call a tool, but fails to actually call one. The mismatch ranges from 26 to 54%, and it concentrates entirely in the cognition-to-action transition, not in cognition itself.<br>
<br>
In other words, the model usually knows it should call the tool.<br>
<br>
The internal probe direction is decodable. But the late-layer last-token regime rotates that signal nearly orthogonal to the action it produces.</p>

- **Why it matters**: If you've been A/B testing tool prompts and hitting weird ceilings, the interpretability result is a better explanation than "bad prompting." If you're picking models from agent leaderboards, the Evaluation Trap paper is the audit checklist. Operationally: (1) add harness-level evals that separate *intent to act* from *action taken*, (2) stop treating leaderboard rank as capability proof without stating your implicit theory, (3) treat grep/harness papers and tool-geometry papers as one stack — same agent, different failure layer.
