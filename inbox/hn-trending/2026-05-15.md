### 5. A Claude Code & Codex Skill for Deliberate Skill Development

- **Points**: 216
- **Comments**: 46
- **URL**: https://github.com/DrCatHicks/learning-opportunities
- **HN**: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48130679

**Summary**

A Claude/Codex Skill that hooks into post-commit events and asks the developer if they'd like a 10–15 minute targeted exercise on the patterns just touched, framing the agent as a learning coach rather than just an autocomplete.

**Key discussion**

- A sharp critique: the entire skill reduces to a single bash hook injecting a one-paragraph prompt — questioning whether "skills" need this much packaging at all.
- Several commenters note many official Anthropic skills are *prompts that beg the model to do better* rather than concrete instructions, prompting a debate on whether skills are documentation, configuration, or motivational text.
- A widely upvoted comment formalizes "skill debt": after sessions of blindly accepting agent output, you can no longer write your own `CLAUDE.md` because you've forgotten how the codebase works — invisible in diffs, fatal when the agent fails.

**Why it matters**

Two collisions in one thread: (a) **what a "Skill" actually is** (prompt? plugin? doc?) is still genuinely unsettled, and (b) the community is starting to articulate **skill debt as a measurable engineering risk** — a needed counterweight to "productivity uplift" benchmarks.

### 6. The AI Zombification of Universities

- **Points**: 164
- **Comments**: 162
- **URL**: https://www.thenewcritic.com/p/the-great-zombification
- **HN**: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=48139355

**Summary**

A polemic arguing that LLMs are accelerating the conversion of universities from places-of-thinking into credential mills, since students can now satisfy assessments without engaging with the material at all.

**Key discussion**

- A repeated, almost unanimous prescription: **bring back proctored, in-person, no-tech exams**. Cheating predates AI — the cost just went down — and the institutional response is overdue.
- A counter-frame: the rot is upstream of AI. If "education = certification = job," AI is just the most efficient way to play that game, and penalizing AI use without changing what's measured will make things worse.
- Nostalgia for orientation speeches like UChicago's "this education will not be useful" — i.e., the institutional case for non-instrumental learning.

**Why it matters**

For anyone building **edtech or hiring signals**, the takeaway is that university transcripts are quietly losing information value, opening room for new credentialing primitives (proctored exams-as-a-service, verified portfolios, on-site work trials).
